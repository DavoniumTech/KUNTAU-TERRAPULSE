# KUNTAU TP — Explaining the October NISAR Observations to Farmers

## A simple explanation

A radar satellite sends microwave signals towards the ground and measures the energy that returns. The return can be influenced by the surface and by how the satellite observes it. It is not the same as a normal camera photograph.

The two KUNTAU TP test observations are from 8 October and 20 October 2026. The team is checking whether the measurements can be compared reliably. Until that check is complete, the difference is exploratory.

## What the current evidence can say

- We have two radar observations collected on different dates.
- The two products have different polarisation configurations: SHSH and DHDH.
- Both product records include an HH channel, but a shared channel does not by itself prove that the values can be compared as a reliable change measurement.

## What the current evidence cannot say

Do not tell farmers that the map proves:

- their crops are healthy or unhealthy;
- crops have been damaged;
- a field has flooded;
- soil moisture increased or decreased;
- irrigation is needed;
- a farmer should plant, harvest, or change a farming practice.

Those conclusions require a validated measurement approach and appropriate independent evidence. Depending on the question, that may include field observations, rainfall data, crop calendars, soil information, and local agricultural expertise.

## Recommended wording in the app

“Radar measurements from these two dates look different in some places. Our team is checking whether the observations are suitable for a reliable comparison. This result is not a diagnosis of crop health, flood damage, or soil moisture. Please do not make a farming decision from this map alone.”

## A farmer-centred question for the team

“What information would help a farmer decide what to inspect in the field—and what additional evidence would be needed before making a decision?”
